# Fajita verbal identity

Version 1.0 · Phase 1 · Internal document. Customer-facing lines are marked as such; everything else is guidance.

The written product name is always **Fajita**. Never FAJITA as a stylistic default, never fajita.io in prose (the domain is not the name), never a stylization that hurts readability. The wordmark handles visual expression; running text stays plain.

## Copy standards

All customer-facing Fajita copy must be clear, specific, economical, visual, benefit-led, conversational, technically accurate, confident without exaggeration, and slightly playful only where the reader is having a good day.

Voice mechanics (inherits `draper-honeycopy.mdc` and `voice-and-boundaries.mdc`):

- Present tense, active voice. Products do things; they do not "empower."
- Short lines that land. Then silence.
- One sharp image beats three adjectives.
- Numbers over adverbs: "checks every 30 seconds," not "blazingly fast."
- No em dashes anywhere in customer-facing copy. Use a period, a comma, parentheses, or a colon.

### Banned vocabulary

Revolutionize, seamless, leverage, game-changing, cutting-edge, all-in-one, next-generation, supercharge, unlock, harness the power, "in today's fast-paced digital landscape," AI-powered (as decoration), empty enterprise language, unsubstantiated market-leading claims, AI clichés, and more than zero exclamation marks per screen in operational contexts.

### Operational vocabulary (fixed)

Use conventional labels in the product: Monitors, Incidents, Status Pages, Alert Channels, Integrations, Maintenance, Team, Billing, Settings. Status vocabulary: Operational, Degraded, Verifying, Down, Maintenance, Paused, Unknown, Recovering. Never rename these with food terms. The metaphor lives in marketing; the product speaks plainly.

### Where the heat metaphor may appear

| Surface | Heat allowed | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marketing headlines | Yes | Primary home of the metaphor |
| Feature-page copy | Sparingly | One heat line per page maximum |
| Empty states | Light touch | "Nothing cooking" register, never during errors |
| Onboarding | Light touch | Welcome moments only |
| Alert emails | No | Plain, factual, calm |
| Incident copy | No | Guardian only |
| Status pages | No | Customers of customers read these |
| App workflows | No | Operational clarity wins |

## Microcopy register

- Buttons: verb plus outcome. "Start monitoring," "See it work," "Publish update." Never "Learn more," "Submit," or "Click here."
- Errors: what happened, what was kept, what to do. Calm, specific, no blame, no internal detail.
- Empty states: explain the feature or help create the first item. One line of wit maximum, and only outside failure contexts.
- Confirmation: state the result plainly. "Monitor created. First check in 30 seconds."

## Review checklist for new copy

1. Would a tired engineer at 3 a.m. understand it instantly?
2. Does it name something specific the product actually does?
3. Zero banned words, zero em dashes, zero fake urgency?
4. Is the Jester off duty if the reader is having an incident?
5. Does it read aloud like a person, not a deck?
